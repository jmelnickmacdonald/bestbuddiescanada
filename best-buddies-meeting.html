About Best Buddies

Friendship is where it starts.

Best Buddies brings students with and without intellectual and developmental disabilities (IDD) together to build genuine friendships, discover shared interests and contribute to their school community.

1989

Best Buddies began through Anthony Kennedy Shriver. Best Buddies Canada launched in 1993, beginning at York University. Today, chapters bring students together in schools and communities across Canada.

The best friendships grow through shared interests, mutual choice and time together. Everyone brings something to the friendship.

Free for schools. Flexible by design. Supported every step of the way.

Your chapter, your way

Make Best Buddies your own.

There's no single way to run a chapter. The most successful approach is one that feels natural for your students, schedule and school community.

Group Gatherings

Meet over lunch, bring out board games, make art, listen to music or organize something together. Regular gatherings give students opportunities to get comfortable, discover shared interests and build friendships at their own pace.

An easy, welcoming place to begin

One-to-One Friendships

Students can be paired based on shared interests and mutual preferences. Buddy pairs get to know one another through regular contact, with guidance from their Staff Advisor and support from Best Buddies Canada.

Friendships can grow naturally through shared activities, smaller groups and regular time together. Schools can also explore structured buddy relationships where appropriate, with support from Best Buddies Canada.

Connections built on shared interests

Student-Led & Hybrid

Student leaders can welcome members, organize events, plan activities and help with recruitment. A Chapter President or executive team can share responsibilities, while chapters combine leadership, gatherings and buddy friendships.

Even younger students can welcome members, choose activities and help plan projects. Middle school students can take on more responsibility. Chapters can combine group gatherings, leadership and friendships.

Making it manageable

Start with what works. Grow from there.

Best Buddies can fit naturally into your school day. You don't have to create a new schedule or plan something elaborate every month.

Once a month?

That's a great starting point. A simple, predictable lunchtime gathering can give students something to look forward to without adding a major scheduling commitment.

Already have a club?

Build Best Buddies into existing lunch clubs, recess activities, leadership classes or social groups. You may already have the perfect time and space available.

Busy staff?

Planning can be shared with colleagues and students. Our Activity Guides provide ready-to-use ideas, and student leaders can gradually take on more responsibility.

Only a few students?

That's absolutely fine. Start with a smaller group and focus on creating meaningful connections. You can welcome more members as interest grows throughout the year.

Limited supplies?

Use board games, colouring materials, craft supplies, music or activities your school already has. Friendship doesn't require a big activity budget.

Want to do more?

As the chapter grows, students may want to plan outings, organize school events, explore buddy friendships or lead larger inclusion initiatives.

What does the Staff Advisor do? Provide guidance and supervision, support recruitment and registration, help students plan activities and oversee buddy friendships where offered. You don't have to organize everything yourself.

Student-Led Inclusion Projects

Students can lead the way.

Elementary and middle school students can welcome members, choose activities, make seasonal decorations, help plan events and contribute to projects that make their school more welcoming.

Student leaders can organize recruitment, plan events, help with communications and develop school-wide initiatives. Chapters might work alongside student council or leadership classes, or have their own Chapter President and executive team.

Friendship and belonging can become part of everyday school life.

Welcoming every student

Make room for everyone.

Participation doesn't have to look the same for every student. A welcoming chapter makes space for different interests, strengths, preferences and ways of connecting.

Student choice

Give students opportunities to suggest activities, make decisions and share what interests them. Some jump right in, while others prefer to observe or take their time.

Different ways to connect

Friendships can develop through games, music, movement, creative activities or simply enjoying time together. Conversation isn't the only way to connect.

Accessible activities

Think about noise, seating, communication supports, predictable routines and quieter options. Small adaptations can make activities more welcoming for everyone.

Meaningful friendships

Encourage students to discover shared interests, enjoy activities together and build connections through mutual choice. Every student should have opportunities to contribute.

Every student has something to contribute.

Your first steps

Let's get your chapter started!

1
Connect and confirm school approval

Identify a Staff Advisor, confirm principal support and complete the school's Program Application Form.

2
Choose your chapter approach

Find a realistic schedule, an accessible meeting location and a chapter model that feels right for your students.

3
Invite students and families

Share the opportunity through announcements, posters, personal invitations or a casual introductory gathering.

4
Complete registration and consent

We'll provide your school's registration link and help you navigate required student registration, consent and safeguarding procedures.

5
Training and your first gathering

Arrange required training, choose a simple activity and welcome students to their first Best Buddies gathering.

You don't need everything figured out today. We'll walk through the steps together.
