WHAT BEST BUDDIES IS ALL ABOUT

Friendship is where it starts.

Best Buddies brings students with and without intellectual and developmental disabilities (IDD) together to build genuine friendships, discover shared interests and contribute to their school community.

1989

Best Buddies began through Anthony Kennedy Shriver. Best Buddies Canada launched in 1993, beginning at York University.

At its heart, Best Buddies is about reciprocal friendships, not assigning some students to help others.

Free for schools. Flexible by design. Supported every step of the way.

A PROGRAM THAT FITS YOUR SCHOOL

Make Best Buddies your own.

There's no single way to run a chapter. Find an approach that reflects your students, their interests and your school community.

Group Gatherings

Lunch, board games, music, arts, games or shared activities. Regular opportunities for students to spend time together and get to know one another.

A relaxed place to start

One-to-One Friendships

Buddy pairs based on shared interests and mutual preferences, with ongoing guidance from the Staff Advisor and Best Buddies Canada.

Natural friendships can grow through shared activities, smaller groups and regular time together. More structured buddy approaches can also be explored.

Building connections over time

Student-Led & Hybrid

A Chapter President or student executive helps with recruitment, activities and events. Chapters can combine leadership, group gatherings and buddy friendships.

Students help welcome members, choose activities, plan projects and take on age-appropriate leadership responsibilities. Older students can take on more.

Students help shape the chapter

Only a few students with IDD? That's okay. Small chapters can create meaningful friendships, too.

A REALISTIC COMMITMENT

Make it work with your schedule.

You don't need another complicated program to manage. Find a rhythm your school can sustain.

When do we meet?

Monthly or twice-monthly gatherings can be a perfectly reasonable place to start.

Lunchtime, recess, regular club time or activities built around existing school events can all work.

What does the Staff Advisor do?

Provide guidance and supervision, support recruitment and registration, help activities run smoothly and oversee buddy friendships where applicable.

You're the steady adult support, not the sole organizer of everything.

Who does the planning?

Planning can be shared among staff and students. Ready-to-use activities make it easier.

Interested students can gradually take on more responsibilities, with appropriate support.

You provide a welcoming space and guidance. We provide training, resources and ongoing support to help make it manageable.
BUILDING ON WHAT WORKS

You're already doing great things.

Best Buddies can grow from what's already happening at your school. Explore a few possibilities.

No need to reinvent the school day. Start with one simple opportunity for friendship.

BUILDING A CULTURE OF BELONGING

Beyond the chapter.

Best Buddies can complement student council, leadership classes, school clubs and Unified Sports, while helping create a wider culture of care, friendship and belonging.

Students can lead the way.

Elementary and middle school students can help choose activities, welcome members, organize projects and contribute ideas. Older students may take on more formal chapter responsibilities.

Student leaders can organize recruitment, events, communications and school-wide initiatives. A Chapter President and student executive can help coordinate the work.

The friendships that begin in a chapter can influence the culture of an entire school.

INCLUSION IN PRACTICE

Make room for everyone.

Participation doesn't have to look the same for every student. Inclusion means creating opportunities for everyone to feel connected, valued and part of the group.

Student choice

Invite students to share interests and preferences. Some may jump right in, while others need time.

Different ways to connect

Friendship can develop through games, music, shared activities, movement or simply time together.

Accessible activities

Consider noise, seating, communication supports, predictable routines and quieter options.

Mutual friendships

Shared interests, mutual preferences and opportunities for everyone to contribute are what make friendships meaningful.

Every student has something to contribute.
