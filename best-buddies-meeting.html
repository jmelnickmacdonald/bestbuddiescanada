About Best Buddies

Friendship is where it starts.

Best Buddies brings students with and without intellectual and developmental disabilities (IDD) together to build genuine friendships, discover shared interests and contribute to their school community.

1989

Best Buddies began through Anthony Kennedy Shriver. Best Buddies Canada launched in 1993. Today, chapters bring students together in schools and communities across Canada.

Friendships grow through shared interests, mutual choice and time together. Every student has something to contribute.

Free for schools. Flexible by design. Supported every step of the way.

Building on what's already working

Already doing something wonderful? Let's build on it.

Many schools already have excellent inclusion, friendship and leadership initiatives. Best Buddies can complement that work rather than replace it.

Keep what works.

Your existing students, meeting times, favourite activities and the relationships already developing can be a wonderful foundation.

If an existing program aligns with Best Buddies, we can explore incorporating it into a chapter rather than creating something completely separate.

Gain something more.

Your school gains practical resources, training, a dedicated Program Advisor and meaningful student leadership opportunities.

You'll also be connected to a network of Best Buddies chapters across Canada, with opportunities to exchange ideas, celebrate and collaborate.

What Best Buddies brings: An intentional focus on ongoing friendships, with the support and wider community to help those connections grow.
Your chapter, your way

Make Best Buddies your own.

Chapters can take different forms depending on student interests and what works within the school community.

Group Gatherings

Students spend time together through games, music, art, lunch or shared projects. Regular gatherings help students get to know one another and discover common interests.

A welcoming place to begin

One-to-One Friendships

Students can be paired based on shared interests and mutual preferences. Buddy pairs build relationships through regular contact, with Staff Advisor guidance and Best Buddies support.

Students build friendships through shared activities, small groups and familiar opportunities to connect. Structured buddy relationships may also be explored where appropriate.

Friendships shaped by student choice

Student-Led & Hybrid

Student leaders can welcome members, organize activities, support recruitment and plan events. Chapters may combine group gatherings, buddy pairs and a Chapter President or executive team.

Students can help choose activities, welcome members and organize projects. Middle school students can take on more responsibility through student leadership roles and shared planning.

Making it work at your school

You may already have the perfect place to start.

Best Buddies can bring students with and without IDD together through everyday routines, shared responsibilities and existing school activities.

Lunch & social time

How it fits

Use a classroom, existing lunch club, cafeteria or recess program.

What it adds

Familiar friends, regular connections and more opportunities for students to enjoy school life together.

School jobs & shared projects

How it fits

Buddies and Peer Buddies share shifts on coffee carts, snack deliveries, school stores or other routines.

What it adds

Friendship through teamwork, shared responsibilities and contributions to school life.

Student council & leadership

How it fits

Students work together to prepare dances, fundraisers, seasonal displays, assemblies and school events.

What it adds

Shared decisions, leadership skills, friendships and meaningful opportunities to contribute.

Clubs, classes & existing programs

How it fits

Build on art clubs, classroom partnerships, peer programs, leadership classes or Unified Sports.

What it adds

More opportunities to get to know one another and develop friendships beyond the original activity.

The Best Buddies connection: Regular opportunities for students to participate as peers, build friendships and contribute together, supported by an official chapter, Staff Advisor and Best Buddies Canada.

The practical side

A manageable commitment. Shared responsibility.

You provide guidance and supervision. Students help bring the chapter to life, and our team supports you along the way.

Finding a rhythm

Start with a monthly or twice-monthly gathering, or build on activities that already take place throughout the school week.

Choose a schedule and accessible space that work for your school. Consistency matters more than elaborate plans.

The Staff Advisor

Provides guidance and supervision, supports recruitment and registration, helps activities run smoothly and supports buddy friendships where applicable.

You are the adult supporting the chapter, not necessarily the person planning every single activity.

Students sharing the work

Students can help choose activities, welcome members, prepare materials and organize gatherings.

Younger students can take on manageable roles, while middle school students may develop a more formal leadership team.

A Chapter President or executive team can coordinate planning, recruitment and events with Staff Advisor support.

Best Buddies Canada helps with the rest: Ready-to-use activities, training, recruitment resources, accessibility ideas, chapter guidance and a Program Advisor available throughout the school year.

Your first steps

Getting started, together.

1
Connect your school

Confirm principal approval, identify a Staff Advisor and complete the school's Program Application Form.

2
Choose your chapter approach

Decide on a meeting time, accessible location and the type of chapter that fits your students. Existing programs may be a natural starting point.

3
Invite and register students

Share your school's unique registration link with families. Parents or guardians register participating Buddies and Peer Buddies.

4
Complete chapter requirements

We'll guide you through registration review, the chapter roster, consent, required safeguarding procedures and training.

5
Bring your chapter together

Hold a Meet & Greet, enjoy your first activity and start building friendships. We'll stay connected throughout the year.

A few practical steps help establish the chapter and support safe, meaningful participation. You won't have to navigate them alone.
