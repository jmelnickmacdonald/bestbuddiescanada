WHAT BEST BUDDIES IS ALL ABOUT

Friendship is where it starts.

Best Buddies brings students with and without intellectual and developmental disabilities (IDD) together to build genuine friendships, discover shared interests and contribute to their school community.

1989

Best Buddies began through Anthony Kennedy Shriver. Best Buddies Canada launched in 1993, beginning at York University.

At its heart, Best Buddies is about reciprocal friendships, not assigning some students to help others.

Free for schools. Flexible by design. Supported every step of the way.

A PROGRAM THAT FITS YOUR SCHOOL

Make Best Buddies your own.

There's no single way to run a chapter. Find an approach that reflects your students, their interests and your school community.

Group Gatherings

Lunch, board games, music, arts, games or shared activities. Familiar, regular opportunities for students to get to know one another.

A relaxed place to start

One-to-One Friendships

Buddy pairs based on shared interests and mutual preferences, with guidance from the Staff Advisor and Best Buddies Canada.

Friendships can develop naturally through shared activities, smaller groups and regular time together. Structured buddy approaches may also be explored.

Building connections over time

Student-Led & Hybrid

Chapter Presidents and executive teams can help with recruitment, activities and events. Chapters can combine leadership, gatherings and buddy friendships.

Students help welcome members, choose activities, plan projects and share their ideas. Middle school students can take on greater leadership roles.

Students help shape the chapter

A REALISTIC COMMITMENT

Start with what works. Grow from there.

There are lots of ways to make room for friendship without adding something complicated to your school day.

Once a month?

A simple lunchtime gathering can be a wonderful place to start.

Already have a club?

Build Best Buddies into lunch clubs, recess, student council or established school activities.

Busy staff?

Share planning with colleagues and students. Use our ready-to-go materials.

Only a few students?

Start with a smaller group. Meaningful friendships don't require a large chapter.

Limited supplies?

Use the games, craft materials and spaces you already have.

Want to do more?

Add student leadership, outings, buddy friendships or school-wide projects over time.

What does the Staff Advisor do? Provide supervision and guidance, support recruitment and registration, help activities run smoothly and oversee buddy friendships where offered. You don't have to personally plan and organize everything!

INCLUSION IN PRACTICE

Make room for everyone.

Participation doesn't have to look the same for every student. Inclusion means creating opportunities for everyone to feel valued, connected and part of the group.

Student choice

Invite students to share interests, ideas and preferences. Some jump right in; others take time.

Different ways to connect

Friendships can develop through games, music, shared activities or simply enjoying time together.

Accessible activities

Consider noise, seating, communication supports, predictable routines and quieter options.

Mutual friendships

Shared interests, mutual preferences and opportunities for everyone to contribute make friendships meaningful.

Every student has something to contribute.
