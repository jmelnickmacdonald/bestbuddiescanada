About Best Buddies

Friendship is where it starts.

Best Buddies brings students with and without intellectual and developmental disabilities (IDD) together to build genuine friendships, discover shared interests and contribute to their school community.

1989

Best Buddies began through Anthony Kennedy Shriver. Best Buddies Canada launched in 1993. Today, chapters bring students together in schools across Canada.

Friendships grow through shared interests, mutual choice and time together. Every student has something to contribute.

Free for schools. Flexible by design. Supported every step of the way.

Building on what's already working

Already doing something wonderful? Let's build on it.

Many schools already have excellent inclusion, friendship and leadership initiatives. Best Buddies can complement that work rather than replace it.

Keep what works.

Your existing students, meeting times, favourite activities and relationships can be a wonderful foundation.

If an existing initiative aligns with Best Buddies, we can explore incorporating it into a chapter rather than creating something separate.

Gain something more.

Your school gains practical resources, training, a dedicated Program Advisor and student leadership opportunities.

You'll also be connected to Best Buddies chapters across Canada, with opportunities to exchange ideas, celebrate and collaborate.

What Best Buddies brings: An intentional focus on ongoing friendships, with the support and wider community to help those connections grow.
Your chapter, your way

Make Best Buddies your own.

Chapters can take different forms depending on student interests and how the school would like to organize its chapter.

Group Gatherings

Students spend time together through shared activities and group experiences. Members get to know one another, discover common interests and build connections over time.

A welcoming, group-based chapter model

One-to-One Friendships

Students can be paired based on shared interests and mutual preferences. Buddy pairs build relationships through regular contact, with guidance and support from their Staff Advisor.

Students can develop friendships through smaller groups and shared experiences. Schools may also explore structured buddy relationships where appropriate.

Friendships shaped by student choice

Student-Led & Hybrid

A Chapter President or executive team can help shape the chapter, coordinate planning and share leadership responsibilities. Schools may combine group gatherings and buddy friendships.

Students help shape the chapter through shared decisions and age-appropriate leadership. Middle school chapters may also develop more formal student leadership teams.

Students have a meaningful voice

Making it work at your school

You may already have the perfect place to start.

Best Buddies can grow from familiar routines and activities already happening throughout your school.

Lunch & social time

How it fits

Open classrooms, lunchtime groups, cafeterias or recess programs.

What it adds

Familiar friends, regular connections and opportunities for social belonging.

School jobs & projects

How it fits

Students share shifts on coffee carts, snack deliveries, school stores or other routines.

What it adds

Friendship through teamwork, shared responsibilities and meaningful contributions.

Student council & leadership

How it fits

Students collaborate on dances, fundraisers, displays and school events.

What it adds

Shared decisions, leadership opportunities and new friendships.

Existing clubs & programs

How it fits

Art clubs, classroom partnerships, peer programs, leadership classes or Unified Sports.

What it adds

More opportunities for relationships to develop beyond the original activity.

The Best Buddies connection: Students with and without IDD have regular opportunities to connect, contribute and develop friendships, with school staff and Best Buddies Canada supporting the chapter.

The practical side

A manageable commitment. Shared responsibility.

The chapter needs staff guidance and supervision, but students can increasingly take ownership of what happens.

Finding a rhythm

Some chapters meet once or twice a month. Others build friendship into activities already taking place during the week.

Choose a realistic schedule, accessible space and level of involvement your school can sustain.

The Staff Advisor

Guides and supervises the chapter, supports recruitment and registration, and helps students plan inclusive activities.

For one-to-one models, the advisor also supports matching and ongoing buddy friendships with help from Best Buddies Canada.

Students taking the lead

Students can welcome members, choose activities, prepare materials and contribute ideas.

Elementary students take on age-appropriate roles; middle school students may develop a leadership team.

High school executives can coordinate events, recruitment and chapter planning, supported by their Staff Advisor.

Best Buddies Canada supports your chapter with training, activity ideas, recruitment materials, planning guidance and a Program Advisor throughout the year.
Building a culture of belonging

Friendship can reach beyond the chapter.

Students can work together to create experiences and initiatives that help the whole school feel more welcoming and connected.

Mix-It-Up Lunch

Plan an optional lunch with activities or shared-interest tables that help classmates connect outside their usual circles.

Friendship & Belonging Campaigns

Create friendship challenges, a belonging wall, school announcements or a themed inclusion week.

School Events

Work with student council on dances, decorations, fundraisers, spirit days or assemblies, sharing decisions and responsibilities.

Community Projects

Organize a mural, welcoming initiative, service project or Youth Friendship Walk inspired by students' interests.

Students help lead the way.

Younger students can contribute ideas, create materials, welcome classmates and help plan activities. Middle school students can take on larger leadership roles.

Chapter members can partner with student council or leadership classes to plan, organize and promote initiatives, with students with and without IDD making decisions together.

Your first steps

Getting started, together.

1
Connect your school

Confirm principal approval, identify a Staff Advisor and complete the Program Application Form.

2
Choose your chapter approach

Decide on a meeting time, accessible location and chapter model that fit your school.

3
Invite and register students

Share your unique school registration link with families. Parents or guardians register participating Buddies and Peer Buddies.

4
Complete chapter requirements

We'll help with registration review, the chapter roster, consent, applicable safeguarding requirements and training.

5
Bring your chapter together

Host a Meet & Greet, enjoy your first activity and begin building friendships.

We'll walk through the process together and stay connected throughout the school year.
