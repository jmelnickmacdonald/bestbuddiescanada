Getting to know Best Buddies

Friendship is where it starts.

Best Buddies Canada is part of an international organization dedicated to creating meaningful friendships and more inclusive communities. In schools, we bring students with and without intellectual and developmental disabilities (IDD) together to build genuine friendships through shared interests, activities and time together.

1989

Founded by Anthony Kennedy Shriver in the United States, following the first chapter at Georgetown University in 1987. Best Buddies came to Canada in 1993, with its first chapter at York University.

Those friendships can continue beyond chapter gatherings, into lunchtime, hallways, classrooms and everyday school life.

Chapter life

What could a chapter look like?

Chapters can include group gatherings, buddy friendships, student leadership or a combination of approaches.

Group gatherings

Students connect through games, lunch, creative activities or shared projects, with regular opportunities to get to know one another.

Buddy friendships

Buddy pairs are guided by shared interests and mutual preferences, with regular contact and support from the Staff Advisor.

Friendships can develop through small groups and familiar activities. Structured buddy pairings may also be explored.

Student-led or a mix

Student executives help plan gatherings, welcome members, organize events and lead projects alongside other chapter activities.

Students help make decisions, welcome members and plan activities. Middle school students can take on more formal leadership roles.

A first gathering could be 30 minutes: 5 welcome · 5 connect · 15 shared activity · 5 plan what's next

The practical side

What does it actually involve?

Here's how responsibilities are shared once a chapter gets going.

Your school & Staff Advisor

Confirm school approval, supervise gatherings, invite students, share registration information and help the chapter run smoothly.

For buddy pairs, guide matching and support ongoing friendships.

Your students

Choose activities, welcome new members, plan projects and enjoy getting to know one another. Every student has a voice.

Elementary students can share small jobs. Middle school students may form a leadership team.

A Chapter President or student executive can support planning, recruitment and events.

Best Buddies Canada

Provide registration guidance, required training, ready-to-use materials and ongoing Program Advisor support.

Help with accessible activities, chapter leadership and questions that arise.

Finding a rhythm: Monthly or twice-monthly lunchtime gatherings are common starting points. Other schools connect through existing clubs, classes or school routines.
School-wide belonging

Students can shape the whole school.

Chapter members can help shape school life alongside student council, classes and other clubs. Their ideas and leadership can reach far beyond a chapter meeting.

Mix-It-Up Lunch

Friendship & belonging week

A belonging wall, announcements, friendship challenges or a student-created campaign.

School events

Plan dances, decorate, help with fundraising, promote events and welcome participants together.

A project for the community

A mural, garden, service project or school-based Youth Friendship Walk.

What students gain: Opportunities to plan, lead, contribute and meet classmates beyond their usual circles, while strengthening school-wide belonging.

If your school is interested

Here's what comes next.

Your Program Advisor will guide you through the steps and share the relevant resources.

1
Confirm a school contact and approval.

Identify the Staff Advisor, confirm principal approval and complete the school's Program Application Form (PAF).

2
Decide where it will fit.

Choose a chapter approach, time and space. Your Program Advisor will share the relevant Quick Start Guide and checklist.

3
Invite and register students.

Best Buddies provides a school-specific registration link for Buddies and Peer Buddies. The chapter roster is a separate record.

4
Get ready for your first gathering.

Work through required consent, safeguarding and training, then plan the first Meet & Greet.

A good question to start with:

What approach feels promising at your school, and who should be involved?

Your Program Advisor can help identify the next step and share the appropriate links and resources.
