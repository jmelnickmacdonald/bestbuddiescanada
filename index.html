Why Best Buddies matters

Every student deserves to feel known, included, and part of their school community.

Best Buddies brings together students with and without intellectual and developmental disabilities through friendship clubs, one-to-one friendships, group activities, and student leadership.

Students spend time together, build friendships, and get to know one another beyond the roles they may usually have at school. For students with intellectual and developmental disabilities, that can mean more opportunities for friendship and social connection. For their peers, it can open the door to new relationships, new perspectives, and leadership.

Over time, those relationships can become part of the wider school culture, shaping how students include one another in classrooms, hallways, activities, and events.

Built to fit your school

A flexible program that can grow with your school.

Best Buddies does not have to look exactly the same everywhere. Chapters can reflect the age of the students, the school schedule, student interests, and the people involved.

Elementary + Middle School

A friendship club built around time together.

Students come together through games, activities, lunch, conversation, and shared experiences. At the middle-school level, chapters can begin to foster friendships between students with and without intellectual and developmental disabilities.

  • Group-focused and social
  • Led by school staff with Best Buddies support
  • Program and activity resources provided
  • Flexible enough to reflect the school community
High School

Friendship and leadership grow together.

High-school chapters can include one-to-one Buddy and Peer Buddy friendships, whole-group activities, and a student executive working alongside a staff advisor.

  • One-to-one and group friendships
  • Student-led chapter activities and events
  • Opportunities to build confidence and leadership
  • Training, guidance, and ongoing support
What schools actually do

A chapter can start simply and grow with your school.

A Best Buddies chapter does not need to be complicated.

At its simplest, a school identifies a staff advisor, brings interested students together, and creates regular opportunities for friendship, activities, and time together.

At the high-school level, students can take on more of the leadership through a chapter executive, with staff and Best Buddies supporting them along the way.

For educators

Your role is to help make the space possible.

You do not need to invent a program from scratch. Teacher advisors help students get started, provide a consistent school contact, and support the chapter as it grows.

Best Buddies provides resources, ideas, training, and ongoing support so the chapter can fit within the realities of your school year.

For students

Students help shape the chapter.

Students help shape activities, welcome members, build friendships, and take on leadership in ways that suit their age and school community.

At the high-school level, this can include a student executive, one-to-one friendships, group activities, and opportunities to connect with other Best Buddies leaders.

“It teaches the leaders about compassion and accountability.”— Tina Limarzi, Teacher and Best Buddies Staff Advisor
Student leadership

High-school students can take on real leadership.

At the high-school level, Best Buddies creates space for students to take an active role in shaping a more inclusive school community.

Student leaders can organize activities, welcome new members, bring people together, and help make inclusion something students actively participate in.

Best Buddies provides training, guidance, and support to help students grow into those roles with confidence.

We’re also looking at new ways to strengthen the student leadership experience this school year — creating more opportunities for students to connect beyond their own chapter, exchange ideas, learn from one another, and feel part of a wider Best Buddies community.

Support for educators

You have support along the way.

A strong chapter still has to fit within a very full school year.

Best Buddies provides resources, ideas, training, and ongoing support so teacher advisors have somewhere to turn when they need help planning a meeting, choosing an activity, supporting student leaders, or deciding what comes next.

Beyond the chapter

Inclusion becomes stronger when students help create it.

Best Buddies gives schools a way to turn a commitment to inclusion into something students experience in everyday school life.

Over time, the impact can move beyond chapter meetings. Students know one another in the hallway. They include one another in activities. They become more comfortable with difference. Student leaders carry what they learn about friendship, communication, and inclusion into the rest of school life.

What starts in a chapter can carry into the rest of school life.

How to get involved

There are a few simple ways to help Best Buddies grow.

Teachers and school staff: If you think Best Buddies could be a fit for your school, start a conversation with us. We can walk you through the model, answer questions, and help you think about what would work in your setting.

Principals and school leaders: Help identify staff and students who may be interested, and support a chapter in finding a workable place in school life.

School boards and regional teams: Help us understand your priorities, connect us with schools that may be interested, and identify where Best Buddies could support work already happening around inclusion, belonging, student leadership, and participation.
