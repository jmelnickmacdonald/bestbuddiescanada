Why Best Buddies matters

Every student deserves to feel known, included, and part of their school community.

Best Buddies brings together students with and without intellectual and developmental disabilities through friendship clubs, one-to-one friendships, group activities, and student leadership.

Students spend time together and get to know one another beyond the roles they may usually have at school. For students with intellectual and developmental disabilities, that can create more opportunities for friendship and social connection. For their peers, it can open the door to new relationships, new perspectives, and leadership.

Over time, those relationships can become part of the wider school culture, shaping how students include one another in classrooms, hallways, activities, and events.

Friendship

Students have regular opportunities to spend time together, discover shared interests, and build relationships that can continue beyond chapter meetings.

Belonging

Best Buddies creates more ways for students to feel welcomed, recognized, and part of the social life of their school.

Leadership

Students can help plan activities, welcome new members, take responsibility, and contribute to the culture around them.

School culture

Inclusion becomes something students practise through the way they know, include, and support one another every day.

Built to fit your school

A flexible program with room to grow.

Best Buddies does not have to look exactly the same everywhere. Chapters can reflect the age of the students, the school schedule, student interests, and the people involved.

Elementary & Middle School

Friendship grows together.

Students come together through games, activities, lunch, conversation, and shared experiences. Chapters are group-focused, social, and supported by school staff and Best Buddies Canada.

High School

Friendship and leadership grow together.

High-school chapters can include one-to-one Buddy and Peer Buddy friendships, group activities, and a student executive working alongside a staff advisor.

What schools actually do

Start with the people who want to make it happen.

A Best Buddies chapter can begin simply. A school identifies a staff advisor, brings interested students together, and creates regular opportunities for friendship and time together.

Teacher advisors provide a consistent point of contact and help students get established, but they are not expected to invent the program on their own. Best Buddies provides resources, ideas, training, and ongoing support as the chapter develops.

At the high-school level, students can take on more responsibility through a chapter executive, helping to plan activities, welcome new members, and shape the direction of their chapter.

Student leadership

High-school students can help lead the way.

Best Buddies gives students a place to take on responsibility while doing something that matters in their own school community. Student leaders can help organize chapter activities, welcome new members, support friendships, and bring other students into the chapter.

Best Buddies provides training, guidance, and ongoing support as students grow into those roles. We are also looking at new ways to strengthen the student leadership experience this school year, including more opportunities for students to connect beyond their own chapter, exchange ideas, and learn from one another.

“It teaches the leaders about compassion and accountability.”— Tina Limarzi, Teacher and Best Buddies Staff Advisor
For school boards and regional teams

Help us understand where Best Buddies can add value.

Every school system has its own priorities, communities, strengths, and pressures. We want to understand what is already happening across your schools and where Best Buddies may complement that work.

Your support can help us connect with interested schools, strengthen chapters that are already operating, and identify opportunities where friendship, belonging, student leadership, and inclusive participation align with your goals.

We are not looking for every school to use the same model. We would rather begin with a conversation and build from what makes sense in your region.
