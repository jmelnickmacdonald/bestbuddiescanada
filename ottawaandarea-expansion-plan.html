School outreach

I do not want to start with a mass email to every Ottawa school. Board referrals and existing relationships should have a chance to produce warmer leads first.

Build the list

20–30 possible schools

  • Current inclusion / peer-support work
  • Unified Sports or inclusive athletics
  • Strong student leadership
  • Learning Support / Special Education staff who are active in school life
  • Lunch clubs, peer mentoring, leadership programs, or similar existing structures
  • Schools referred by board staff, families, or current chapters
Outreach order

Warm first, cold second

  • Board introduction or direct referral
  • Referral from an existing chapter / teacher advisor
  • Parent or student interest that suggests a school may be receptive
  • Direct outreach to selected schools after the first round of board conversations

Current and former chapters

This is mainly a housekeeping and relationship piece. I need a clearer picture of what is active, what is quiet, and where there is an easy opportunity to re-engage.

Current chapters

Identify which chapters are healthy and which ones need more contact or support.

Dormant / quiet chapters

Look at why they stopped. Staff turnover or leadership loss may be fixable; lack of interest may not be.

Reference chapters

Identify 2–3 local schools that are comfortable being examples when a board or new school asks what Best Buddies looks like in practice.

For the October trip, a visit to one or two current chapters would be useful if schedules line up. This is more valuable if it helps with relationship-building or gives us a stronger local example, rather than visiting just to visit.

Parents, families, and community organizations

The goal here is awareness and local intelligence. Parents and community groups can help identify where interest already exists and connect Best Buddies with families, but they should not be put in the position of pressuring schools to start a chapter.

Parent / board groups

  • OCDSB Parent Involvement Committee
  • OCDSB SEAC
  • OCSB CSPA / Parent Involvement Committee
  • OCSB SEAC
  • CDSBEO Parent Involvement Committee
  • School councils where there is clear interest

Community organizations to explore

  • Ottawa Network for Education
  • LiveWorkPlay
  • Down Syndrome Association – National Capital Region
  • ABLE2
  • Ottawa-Carleton Lifeskills
  • Inclusion Ottawa / Children’s Inclusion Support Services, likely as a longer-term connection
Not all of these need to become formal partners. Some may simply be useful conversations, referral sources, or people who understand the local landscape better than we do.

Teacher and educator groups

My first choice is still to reach educators through board-supported groups, because those conversations are closer to actual school programming. Professional associations and union locals may be useful later for awareness, PD, or finding teacher champions.

First
  • Learning Support / Special Education teacher networks
  • Student Success teachers
  • Guidance / student-services staff
  • Department heads
  • Principal / vice-principal groups
  • Board equity, inclusion, belonging, and well-being teams
Later / if useful
  • Ottawa-Carleton ETFO
  • OSSTF District 25
  • Ottawa Catholic Teachers / OECTA
  • Ontario Council for Exceptional Children
  • Faculty of Education / Special Education AQ networks

Post-secondary

uOttawa

There is already a Best Buddies chapter. I need to reconnect with the current leaders, understand how the year is going, and make sure there are strong students ready to take over as the current executive gets closer to graduating or moving on.

Carleton

New expansion opportunity. Identify a student champion and also connect with student engagement / community-engagement staff so the chapter is not dependent on finding one person by chance.

Algonquin

Explore whether there is a fit through student clubs, volunteering, or student engagement. Treat this as an initial conversation rather than assuming a chapter is ready to launch.
