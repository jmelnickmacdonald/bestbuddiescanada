What Best Buddies brings

Friendship can make a campus feel a lot more connected.

Best Buddies creates more chances for people with and without intellectual and developmental disabilities to meet, spend time together, and build real friendships.

People bring their own interests, personalities, ideas, and strengths to the chapter. Time together can look like coffee, games, campus events, outings, conversation, or whatever the group genuinely enjoys.

A strong chapter can become an inclusion anchor on campus: a place where people know each other, show up, and feel part of something.

Friendship

More opportunities to meet, spend time together, find common ground, and let relationships grow.

Community

A connection between campus and the wider community that feels social, welcoming, and genuine.

Belonging

A chapter culture where people are known, included, and encouraged to contribute in their own way.

The goal is not one group doing something for another. It is people getting to know one another and building a chapter together.

Student leadership

Lead something real.

A Best Buddies executive is more than a title. Student leaders help shape the chapter, build the team around it, and keep it moving throughout the year.

You might step in as a Chapter President or Co-President, take ownership of a specific part of the chapter, or help build an executive around the strengths of the people involved.

There’s room to build a leadership team that works.

Executive responsibilities can be divided across the team, so one person is not carrying the whole chapter.

Useful well beyond campus

What you learn here goes with you.

Best Buddies starts with friendship. The experience of building relationships, leading a team, and creating a chapter where people feel welcome can also shape how you work with people long after university or college.

Heading into a people-focused career?

Education, medicine, nursing, social work, psychology, occupational therapy, physiotherapy, recreation, and plenty of other fields all involve working with people whose experiences and communication styles may differ from your own.

Best Buddies gives you time to know people outside a classroom, clinical, or service relationship. Listening, communicating, making space for different ways of participating, and getting comfortable with difference all matter.

More interested in building and organizing?

Business, marketing, communications, event management, fundraising, and student leadership all have a place here too.

Running a chapter means working with people, solving problems, planning ahead, communicating clearly, and following through. Those skills are useful almost anywhere.

Still figuring out what comes next? That’s fine too. Friendship, community, and having something meaningful to be part of are good reasons on their own.
How chapters work

Your chapter can take shape around the people in it.

University and college chapters can use a one-to-one friendship model, a group model, or build chapter activities around the format that fits the people involved.

One-to-one friendships

Buddy pairs are matched around shared interests, stay in touch regularly, and spend time together throughout the year while taking part in wider chapter activities.

Group-based chapter

Members spend time together through regular group activities such as games, outings, campus events, celebrations, or other plans shaped by the chapter.

The strongest chapters grow around the people involved, not around a rigid template.
